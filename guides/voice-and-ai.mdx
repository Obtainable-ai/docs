Three features that speed up capture and drafting: **dictating** a thought instead of typing it, **listening** to a note or source read aloud, and the **AI editor** built into notes.

## Dictate a thought

Capture a thought by voice from the **What's on your mind?** box in the [Thoughts](/core-concepts/thoughts) feed.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start recording">
    Click the **microphone** button on the capture box. Recording begins immediately — the box shows **Recording…**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Speak, then stop">
    Say what's on your mind and select **Stop**. PeerNotes shows **Transcribing…** while it turns the audio into text.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review and capture">
    The transcript drops into the box (added on a new line if you'd already typed something). Edit it, pick a [topic](/core-concepts/topics) or the share toggle if you want, then select **Capture Thought**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You can start dictation straight from the collapsed capture box, or from the mic button in its footer once it's open. Closing the box before you stop discards the recording without transcribing it.

<Note>
  Dictation needs microphone access and a browser that supports audio recording. If you've blocked the mic, PeerNotes shows *"Microphone access denied"* — re-allow it in your browser's site settings and try again.
</Note>

## Listen to notes and sources

Any [note](/core-concepts/notes) or [source](/core-concepts/sources) can be read aloud.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the item and select Listen">
    In the top bar of a note or source, select **Listen** (the headphones icon). It synthesizes speech and starts playing — audio streams in, so playback begins before the whole piece is ready.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pause and resume">
    While it's playing, the control becomes **Pause**; paused, it becomes **Resume**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Playback reads the title first, then the body — headings, paragraphs, and lists — with abbreviations expanded and natural pauses. The **voice** and **speed** come from your [Voice preferences](/configuration/user-preferences#voice) (six voices; 0.75× to 2×).

<Tip>
  Listening is a good final pass on a draft note — problems you skim past in text are obvious when you hear them — and a way to get through a long source while you're away from the screen.
</Tip>

## The AI editor

The note editor has AI help built in. Nothing is permanent: whole-note changes apply straight to the draft but undo with `⌘Z`, and selection rewrites are shown for you to accept or discard first.

### Whole-note actions

In the editor toolbar, the **AI** group has:

| Action | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Proofread** | Corrects grammar, spelling, and punctuation, leaving your content and meaning unchanged. |
| **Rewrite** | Improves clarity, structure, and depth while preserving your key ideas. |

Each one replaces the note with the improved version. If it's not what you wanted, press `⌘Z` to undo.

### Rewrite a selection

Select any passage in a note to open the bubble menu, then choose **Rewrite**. Type an instruction — *"make this tighter"*, *"turn this into a list"* — and PeerNotes returns a **Suggestion**. **Accept** to swap it in for the selected text, or **Discard** to keep what you had. (The same menu's **Comment** action is covered in [the Peer Agent](/core-concepts/peer-agent#tag-it-in-a-comment).)

### Draft a new note

When you create a note, the **New Note** sheet can draft the first version for you:

- **Draft with AI** — describe what you want, e.g. *"summarize this week's standup meetings"*.
- **References** — attach [thoughts](/core-concepts/thoughts) and [sources](/core-concepts/sources) for the draft to draw on.
- **Note Style** — pick an existing note whose tone and structure the draft should match.

Leave all three off and you get a blank note. See [Writing notes](/guides/writing-notes) for the full drafting workflow.

<Info>
  The AI editor works from your note and the references you attach — it's the writing-time counterpart to the [Peer Agent](/core-concepts/peer-agent), which you chat with or tag in comments.
</Info>
