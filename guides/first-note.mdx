> Go from a piece of customer feedback to a note your whole team can act on, in one pass.

## Overview

The fastest way to learn PeerNotes is to run a real piece of work through it. This guide follows one: a Customer Success team turning a customer's feedback into shared knowledge. You'll **save a source**, **comment on it where it matters**, and **write a note** that the rest of the team picks up — the same loop you'll repeat every day.

Every step happens inside your [Customer Success workspace](/core-concepts/workspace), so switch to it (or create it) first.

## The path

<Steps>
  <Step title="Save the source">
    You've got a customer's message, a call recording summary, or a churn post-mortem doc.

    - **A web page** (a support-forum thread, a feature request, a competitor's changelog) — use the [web clipper](/features/web-clipper): open the extension on the page and **Save**. The page content and URL land in your [Sources](/core-concepts/sources) feed.
    - **A file** (PDF, doc, transcript) — open **Sources** in the sidebar and upload it.

    Keep it private for now — you'll share the note, not the raw material.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Read it and comment where it matters">
    Open the source in the viewer. When you hit the sentence that matters — the reason a customer is frustrated, the workaround they've built — **select the text** and choose **Comment**.

    - Write what you noticed: *"Third escalation this quarter about export limits."*
    - `@`-mention the teammate who owns that account, or `@PeerNotes Agent` to ask *"has this come up in other sources?"*

    The comment stays attached to that exact passage, so anyone who opens the source later sees your read of it.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Capture a thought or two (optional)">
    While it's fresh, jot a reaction in the **Thoughts** feed — one line is enough: *"Export limits are becoming a churn signal, not just a support annoyance."* PeerNotes links it to the source and files it under a [topic](/core-concepts/topics) automatically.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Turn it into a note">
    Create a note from the **New Note** sheet:

    - Add the source (and any thoughts) as **References**.
    - Give it a title the team will scan for: *"Export limits: churn signal from Q3 escalations"*.
    - Draft it yourself, or use **Draft with AI** with a prompt like *"summarize the pattern across these escalations and recommend a next step"*, then tighten it.
    - Tag a topic (e.g. **Churn** or **Product feedback**) so it sits alongside related thinking.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Share it with the workspace">
    Turn on **Share with workspace**. The note now appears in the **Shared** view of the Notes feed for every member — CS, and anyone from product you've added to the workspace. If you want a specific PM to build on it directly, add them as a [collaborator](/features/collaborators).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Why this order

- **Source first** means every claim in your note traces back to something real a reviewer can check.
- **Comment in context** captures your judgement at the moment you have it, on the exact passage — not paraphrased from memory a day later.
- **Note last** means you're composing from material you've already reacted to, not staring at a blank page.

## Tips

- **Lead with the point.** Put the recommendation or the pattern in the first line of the note — teammates decide from that whether to read on.
- **One idea per note.** "Export limits churn signal" and "Onboarding drop-off" are two notes, not one.
- **Let topics do the filing.** Don't build folders; tag a topic and move on.
- **Share early.** A note marked *draft, feedback welcome* and shared beats a perfect note nobody sees.
