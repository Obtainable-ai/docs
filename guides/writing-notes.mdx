Writing in PeerNotes usually doesn't start with a blank page. It starts with the [thoughts](/core-concepts/thoughts) you've already captured — and a note is where you compile them into something structured. This guide walks through the full writing flow, from selecting thoughts to sharing a finished note.

## Start from your thoughts (recommended)

<Steps>
  <Step title="Review a topic">
    Open the Thoughts feed and filter by the topic you want to write about — or browse the Topics page to see where thinking has accumulated.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select the thoughts that hold up">
    Select each thought you want to include. A floating bar tracks your selection at the bottom of the screen.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Convert to Note">
    Choose **Convert to Note**. Your selected thoughts land in a new note, ready to be shaped into prose.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You can also start a blank note from the Notes feed when you already know what you want to say.

## Write and structure

The editor is built for prose: headings, bullet and numbered lists, bold and italic, and links. Everything saves automatically as you type — close the tab mid-sentence and your draft will be waiting.

It also has AI help built in — **Proofread** and **Rewrite** for the whole note, a **Rewrite** action on any selection, and **Draft with AI** when you create a note. See [the AI editor](/guides/voice-and-ai#the-ai-editor).

A few practices that make notes your peers can actually engage with:

<Tip>
  **Lead with your point.** Put the conclusion, question, or recommendation in the first sentence. Teammates skimming the feed decide from that line whether to open the note.
</Tip>

<Tip>
  **One idea per note.** Focused notes are easier to discuss, easier to find, and easier to share. If you're covering two things, write two notes.
</Tip>

<Tip>
  **Attach your sources.** When a note cites the material it came from, reviewers can check the original rather than taking your word for it. See [Sources](/core-concepts/sources).
</Tip>

<Tip>
  **Ask the assistant before you share.** The [AI assistant](/guides/ai-assistant) will tell you what a careful reader is going to ask. Better to hear it before your teammates do.
</Tip>

## Tag topics

Add [topics](/core-concepts/topics) to keep the note discoverable alongside the related thoughts and notes in your workspace. Thoughts you compiled usually carry their topics with them.

## Share it

Notes start private. When a draft is ready for other eyes, you have two options that stack:

1. **Share with the workspace** — turn on **Share with workspace** from the note's card or the note itself. Every member can now read it in the **Shared** view of the Notes feed and comment on it. Toggle it back off any time.
2. **Add collaborators** — invite specific people by email to *edit* the note with you. Adding a collaborator shares the note with the workspace automatically.

Keep editing after you share — changes are live, and you can unshare whenever you want. See [Sharing & privacy](/collaboration/sharing-notes) for the full picture.

<Info>
  New notes follow your [Sharing preference](/configuration/user-preferences#sharing) — private by default, unless you've set notes to share automatically.
</Info>
